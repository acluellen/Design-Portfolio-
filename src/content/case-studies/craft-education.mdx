## People found Craft, then couldn't tell what to do next.

Craft runs assessments and treatment plans through Hubeta, an AI assistant on WhatsApp, and supports therapists with Hunu, its care platform. The website is the front door to both. Referrals weren't growing beyond Ghana, and Craft's own brief named the reason:

<Quote source="Craft Education project brief">"Users are unable to understand the impact of our product and do not know where the CTA is to know what to do next."</Quote>

At kickoff, Craft's CEO set the bar plainly: "If we get one signup, we have succeeded."

<Callout label="From the brief" variant="strong">Clear user journeys for each product: who it is for and what it does.</Callout>

## Three problems blocked every path.

<CardGrid>
  <Card eyebrow="Heuristic" title="Match between system and the real world">
    The most common problem. Product names like Hunu and Hubeta meant nothing to a first time visitor.
  </Card>
  <Card eyebrow="Heuristic" title="Recognition rather than recall">
    People had to remember where they came from to understand where they were. Picking a role led back to the same general content.
  </Card>
  <Card eyebrow="Heuristic" title="Visibility of system status">
    No clear next step. Get Started sent people to WhatsApp with no warning, and the chat opened with no instructions.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## Every participant hit a wall.

The team asked people to say what the site was for, find what Craft offers, work out what Hunu and Hubeta are, and sign up. Not one participant completed a key task. No parent found the chat button without a prompt, and once shown it, parents took it for site help, not the assessment.

<Split>
  <Quote source="A parent, usability testing">"I'm lost. I don't know what I'm doing."</Quote>
  <Quote source="A provider, usability testing">"It feels like a ghost site. I don't know who I'm sending this to."</Quote>
</Split>

<Figure
  src={journeyParent}
  alt="Journey map of a parent's visit to the original site: screens across the top, then his actions, a line of feelings that drops after he is sent to WhatsApp, his quotes, the heuristic issues and possible solutions"
  caption="A parent's journey through the original site. He arrived hopeful, was sent to WhatsApp without warning, and left still asking who Craft was."
/>

Providers needed proof before they would refer anyone: "I wouldn't refer a patient without knowing what this platform really does." Parents needed a guide. They described years of figuring things out alone:

<Quote source="A parent, interview">"There really is no roadmap… you're on your own"</Quote>

The competitors showed the fix. Each one says plainly who every path is for, keeps reassuring people along the way, and shows partners, numbers and expert voices before asking for any personal information.

### The parent path

Ayisha lands on a homepage that says who Craft is for, with her role first. The parents page explains what happens next, then Hubeta starts right on the page. In about ten minutes she has begun her assessment and learned how Hubeta works by using it. When she finishes, Hubeta sends her to the Learning Center, so she can prepare for the call from Craft's team.

## Testing the redesign

The team tested the mid fidelity prototype with returning participants from the first round, including a therapist. They completed the parent and provider tasks that had stopped everyone before. The therapist's concerns led to one more change: the About page now shows Hubeta in use on a phone, so the tools are easier to tell apart.

<Section>

## Reflection

Craft's product was already helping families on WhatsApp. The website's job was to get people there with enough trust to start, and that came down to plain answers: who this is for, who is behind it, and what happens next. The parents the team interviewed lived in North America. Next, I would test the parent path with families in Ghana, on their phones, since that is how most of Craft's users reach it.

</Section>
