## Translating one school form takes two weeks of notice.

It started with one principal's complaint. The team traced it to a workflow gap: messages translate automatically, and attachments wait on the district translation unit. A family waiting on a special education plan or a health form gets it late, or reads it through their child:

  <Stat value="9 staff" label="for 13,314 English learners" source="OUSD Interpretation and Translation Unit · California Department of Education, 2025 to 2026" />
</StatGroup>

## Two findings changed who the product serves and how it works.

<Shift
  before={{ label: "The original idea", title: "ParentSquare translator" }}

  rows={[

    { label: "Who translates", before: "Third party translation model", after: "AI, checked by an administrator", finding: 1 },
    { label: "Accuracy", before: "Machine output, no human check", after: "Human approval before delivery", finding: 2 },
    { label: "Privacy", before: "Records leave district control", after: "Records stay under district control", finding: 2 },
  ]}
/>

<p class="label">Why it changed</p>

<Finding
  n={1}
  finding="State law puts the translation duty on the district, and only the district's board can sign the contract."
  changes={["Buyer", "Who translates"]}
  sources="Cal. Educ. Code § 48985 · OUSD Board Policy 5124 · Cal. Educ. Code § 17604"
>
  After an engineer and a design consultant pointed the team toward schools, the product lead made the district the buyer. The UX and UI leads rebuilt the flows and screens around school staff.
</Finding>

<Finding
  n={2}
  finding="Unreviewed machine translation of student records creates Title VI and FERPA exposure."
  changes={["Accuracy", "Privacy"]}
  sources="U.S. v. Cherry Creek School District settlement, 2024 · 34 CFR § 99.31(a)(1)(i)(B) · Orange County Dept. of Education RFP 26-01, 2026"
>
  Districts already have a person check vital translations, so the team made that step part of the product. I introduced the review flow, and the UX and UI leads rebuilt the user flow and screens around it, including a verification screen with the source and translation side by side.
</Finding>

Competing school apps tell schools their document translation "is not intended for use with legal documents." AthenaScribe was built for those documents. It flags them on upload and routes them to a person.

## The new design held up in testing, and the queue needed sorting.

The two findings above put a person in charge of approving vital documents. We gave that step to the administrators who would run it and asked two things: can they use it, and does it fit their day?

## A safeguard nobody has time to use is still a delay.

I designed the review step for accuracy first. Testing showed speed matters just as much, so the next version puts a deadline on every vital document, visible to staff and administrators.
