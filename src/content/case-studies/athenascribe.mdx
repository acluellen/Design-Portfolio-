<p class="label">What participants raised</p>

Feedback pointed to grouping health forms, permission slips, and other document types. Testing showed staff sort documents by type, so the queue should too.

<p class="label">What changed in the interface</p>

The proposed revision groups documents by type. It also shows each document’s status, so staff can see the next action, and it makes deadlines visible.

{/* The deadline finding is not confirmed against a session record, so it is stated as design analysis. If a session record shows participants raised it, move it up into "What participants raised". */}

The deadline treatment comes from our design analysis. We are not reporting it as participant feedback.

<p class="label">What remains to be tested</p>

The revised queue is a proposal. Further testing will examine whether staff can find priority documents, understand responsibility, and complete the review process.

## What I contributed and what remains.

I led the user research and contributed product strategy, competitive analysis, and prototyping. The team developed an interactive prototype and presented AthenaScribe at BRIDGEGOOD Demo Day at Block HQ.

I used feature prioritization to connect our findings to three priorities for the first experience. I examined education policy and district guidance, then worked with the product lead to incorporate staff review into the proposed workflow.

Research showed the problem was bigger than the document. The work also included who checks it and who gets it back to the family. Testing showed staff sort documents by type, so the queue should too. A clear flow still has to fit someone’s day.

### Review still depends on people and time.

Routing important documents to staff creates a practical question: how can review fit within the district’s workload without becoming another delay?
