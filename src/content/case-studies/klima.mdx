## People wanted to act on climate change, and they did not trust where to start.

Klima helps people measure their carbon footprint and fund certified projects that offset it. Our brief asked us to meet user needs and product goals together: usability, engagement, retention and movement toward the paid subscription.

What we found is that action starts with trust. That finding shaped the whole redesign.

The team mapped the existing journey screen by screen, from calculating a carbon footprint to subscribing. Confusion clustered around three moments: the numbers at the top of the calculator, the step where people choose projects to fund, and a sign up wall that made people hesitate.

Three of the people who tested the original app also filled in the System Usability Scale. Their scores were 85, 72.5 and 42.5, an average of about 67: easy for some people and hard for others.

### Competitive analysis

I compared Klima with direct, indirect and lateral products: Yayzy, Pawprint, Planet Wild, Garmin and GoFundMe.

- **What worked:** emotional imagery is great for people new to climate action.
- **What was missing:** no game system to build habits, and no reason to come back and act again.

The feature inventory put every product side by side. Klima was the only one in the set with neither real time offsetting nor any game features.

{/* Rebuilt from the Feature Inventory section of the team FigJam (read Oct 8 2026). */}

| Feature | Klima | Yayzy | Pawprint | Planet Wild | Garmin | GoFundMe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dashboard | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | N/A | N/A |
| Carbon footprint calculator | Yes | Yes | Yes | N/A | N/A | N/A |
| Offsetting models | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | N/A | N/A |
| Real time offsetting | Missing | Yes | Missing | Missing | N/A | N/A |
| Gamification | Missing | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Payment flexibility | Yes | Yes | Missing | Yes | N/A | N/A |

Yayzy is a direct competitor. Pawprint and Planet Wild are indirect. Garmin and GoFundMe are lateral: they don't offset carbon, but they keep people coming back with badges, challenges, leaderboards and visible progress.

From the analysis, the biggest chances to stand out were:

1. Combine tracking with game style challenges.
2. Build a community hub with shared progress, leaderboards and badges.
3. Use storytelling and transparency, like Planet Wild and GoFundMe, to build trust.

## Synthesis

We built a persona, Layla: an optimistic beginner in her mid 30s, a mother of two and a marketing manager, who cares about climate change but needs to know where her money goes. Two secondary personas, Peter and Ted, covered the investor and the skeptical activist.

Layla's core problems formed a loop:

1. **Trust:** she is unsure about subscribing when she does not fully understand what the service offers or who is behind it.
2. **Understanding:** she needs to know how her footprint is calculated before she trusts the number.
3. **Impact:** she needs to see the real world result of the projects she funds.
4. **Motivation:** she needs to see progress, or earn recognition, to keep taking climate positive actions.

{/* These are synthesized needs, written as needs rather than quotes (Aaron, Oct 7). */}

### Hypothesis

If we increase trust and transparency in the platform and give people reasons to come back, they will be more likely to donate, keep returning and commit to a sustainable life over the long term.

We planned to read it through user satisfaction in interviews and the System Usability Scale, alongside the brief's product goals: engagement, conversion to a paid plan and retention.

## My focus: motivation after joining

Our feature analysis showed that Klima had no structure for building habits and no incentive to act again. People did not feel their actions were making an impact, and they needed motivation to stay consistent.

Klima's community lived in its Multiply tab. Everything there was a one time action: invite a friend, write to a representative, watch a video. Nothing brought people back the next day.

  caption="Before: Klima's Multiply tab, built around referrals and advocacy."

I designed an Impact Hub, a home for daily action that rewards consistency and turns climate impact into visible progress:

- **Challenges:** daily actions that reduce emissions and build momentum.
- **Streaks:** encourage consistency over time.
- **Badges:** mark milestones and unlock new rewards.
- **Garden:** a virtual garden that grows as you act, so impact becomes something you can see.
- **Community:** Discord built into Klima's existing Multiply feature, to turn a passive community into active participation and lay the groundwork for a fully gamified experience.

### One challenge a day

Inside the hub, the loop is short: pick a challenge, log it, see what it saved, and keep the streak going.

1. **Choose a challenge.** Layla gets picks recommended for her, plus themed sets like Stop Waste and Earth Day. Each card shows how much CO₂ the action saves.
2. **Log it.** Each challenge is tied to the UN Sustainable Development Goals it supports: Responsible Consumption and Production, and Climate Action.
3. **See today's impact.** The saving is put in everyday terms, like driving 2 miles in a gas powered car, and a streak tracker shows the week so far.
4. **Earn a badge.** Seven days in a row unlocks the first badge, Starter Seed.

## What testing showed

The first test ran on the mid fidelity wireframes, one task per stage:

{/* Rebuilt from the Usability Test Summary table on the team FigJam (read Oct 8 2026). */}

| Task | What worked | What caused friction | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Onboarding | The opening visuals made the climate focus clear from the start | Being asked to offset before knowing her footprint felt backwards | Calculate the footprint first, then offer offsetting |
| Exploring projects | The plain explanation of offsetting helped someone new to the idea | Static cards looked clickable | Make it clear which cards are not interactive |
| Gamification | The All Badges screen was clear: she understood it tracked her achievements | A label that read Price Match Breakdown Here was confusing | Replace vague labels with clear, action based ones |

In testing, people said the garden made their actions feel real and rewarding, badges and streaks made them excited about progress, and challenges taught them how to live more sustainably.

The redesign was designed to support engagement, retention and conversion to the paid plan. The team did not have Klima's analytics and the redesign never shipped, so those outcomes were not measured.

## Reflection

The research pointed to one idea: connect what people feel to actions they can see. Each stage of the redesign was built around it, so that Layla meets a platform that is clear, trustworthy and motivating, and feels ready to act.
